Before applying TF-IDF to assess word importance, we first need to tokenize the text — breaking it down into individual words. Then, we normalize it by removing punctuation and capital letters, followed by filtering out stop words such as 'and' or 'the' that do not provide meaningful information.

After the information is prepared, each word is measured by its frequency of appearance in a specific document, known as Term Frequency (TF). A word that appears often in a document receives a higher TF score.

TF(t, d) · = count of t in d ÷ total words in d

Measuring using TF alone is not enough, as some words may appear across multiple documents. To address this, Inverse Document Frequency (IDF) is introduced to calculate the importance of a word by reducing the weight of common words across documents.

A word with a high TF but low IDF — such as 'and' — will have a low overall TF-IDF score.

IDF(t) · = log( N ÷ df(t) )

Finally, by multiplying TF and IDF, we can determine the most relevant words in a document. Higher TF-IDF scores signify greater importance — the word is both frequent in this document and rare across the corpus.

TF-IDF(t, d) · = TF(t, d) × IDF(t)
